6. Research Gap:
From the reviewed literature, it is observed that existing research has extensively explored file
integrity monitoring, host-based intrusion detection, ransomware detection, and cryptographic
techniques for identifying suspicious file activity. Recent studies have introduced advanced
approaches such as real-time monitoring, behavioral analysis, kernel-level monitoring, machine
learning, and similarity-preserving hashing. These approaches can provide strong security
capabilities, but many of them are designed for specific threats or require comparatively complex
monitoring mechanisms and system resources.
A significant gap is the limited focus on simple and lightweight file integrity verification systems
suitable for local and small-scale environments. Many advanced approaches concentrate on
detecting ransomware or broader malicious behavior rather than providing a straightforward
mechanism for users to verify the integrity of selected files and directories. In addition, different
types of file changes, such as modification, addition, deletion, and unchanged status, are not always
presented through a simple and user-oriented integrity report.
The proposed File Integrity Tester aims to address these gaps by developing a lightweight software
solution based on cryptographic hashing and trusted baseline comparison. The system allows
selected files or directories to be scanned, generates cryptographic hash values, stores baseline
information, and performs subsequent scans to identify changes. It classifies files as modified,
added, deleted, or unchanged and presents the results through an integrity report. File metadata
such as path, size, and modification time can also be maintained to provide additional information
during comparison.
